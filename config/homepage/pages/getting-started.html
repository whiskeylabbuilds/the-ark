THE ARK

GETTING STARTED

What each section does, how to use it, and answers to common questions

The Ark is our family's offline library and AI helper. It runs on the small computer in the house and keeps working with no internet: encyclopedias, medical guides, courses, books, maps, notes and a private AI, all reachable from any phone, tablet or laptop on our Wi-Fi.

Open a web browser and go to http://ark.local. That's the dashboard. Every tile on it opens one section.

First steps

  1. Join our Wi-Fi. The Ark is only reachable from inside the house network (or a travel router it's plugged into). It does not need the internet.
  2. Open the dashboard at http://ark.local. On a phone, use your browser's Add to Home Screen so it opens like an app.
  3. Make your own logins where asked. Ask the Ark (AI), the Bookshelf and Courses each have their own account. The very first account created in each one becomes the administrator, so a parent should create theirs first.
  4. Try a search. Open the Reference Library and search for something you know, like water purification or how to splint a broken arm.
Status dots: each tile has a small dot. Orange means that section is running. Red or gray means it's down or not started yet; see the FAQ.

The sections

Reference Library

Kiwix · port 8080
Open ↗

Dozens of complete websites saved for offline use. Pick a book from the main page, then use its search box, or search across everything from the top bar.

What's inside

  • Encyclopedia: all of English Wikipedia (text only, every article in full), Wikibooks (how-to textbooks), Wikisource, Wikivoyage (travel and places).
  • Medical: WikiMed (medical Wikipedia with pictures), LibreTexts Medicine, CDC, Medical Sciences Q&A, plus WikiVet for animals.
  • Survival and preparedness: Ready.gov, water, food preparation, knots, field medicine and post-disaster guides.
  • Fixing things: iFixit repair guides, and Q&A sites for DIY/home improvement, car mechanics, electronics, gardening, cooking, ham radio and the outdoors.
  • Learning: LibreTexts textbooks (biology, chemistry, physics, math, engineering, K-12, trades), and Q&A sites for math, physics and philosophy.
  • Faith and philosophy: Project Gutenberg's philosophy and religion collection, and Q&A sites on Christianity and Bible interpretation.
Tip: search inside one book for better results. Open WikiMed first for medical questions, and iFixit or the DIY site for repairs.

Music

Navidrome · port 4533
Open ↗

Our own music collection, playable in any browser on the Wi-Fi. It works with no internet.

  • Browse by artist, album, genre or playlist, or search. Click a song or album to play it; use the bar at the bottom to pause, skip and set the volume.
  • Each person can have their own login, with their own playlists and favorites. The first account created becomes the administrator; add others under Settings → Users.
  • Phone apps: free Subsonic apps (Symfonium or Substreamer on Android, play:Sub or Amperfy on iPhone) can stream from the Ark and save songs on the phone to play away from home. In the app, add a server: address http://ark.local:4533 plus your Music login.

To add songs, see Add music below.

Ask the Ark (AI)

Open WebUI + Ollama · port 3000
Open ↗

A private AI chat, like ChatGPT, that runs entirely on the Ark. Nothing you type leaves the house.

Choose a model (top-left menu)

ModelBest for
qwen3:8bThe main one. Best at reasoning, math, step-by-step instructions.
llama3.1:8bA second opinion. Ask both if an answer matters.
gemma3:4bFaster, and can look at photos: attach a picture of a plant, a part or a label and ask about it.

  • You can attach a PDF to a chat and ask questions about it. To make a set of PDFs always available, see Teach the AI your documents.
Always double-check: small offline AIs can sound confident and still be wrong. For medicine, doses, electrical work or anything dangerous, confirm in WikiMed, the Reference Library or a manual before acting.

Talk to the Ark (voice)

Whisper listens · Kokoro speaks · all on the Ark
Open ↗

Ask the Ark can listen and talk back, fully offline. Nothing you say leaves the house.

Using it

  • Dictate: tap the microphone in the message box, speak, tap again. Your words appear as text; send them as usual.
  • Hands-free: tap the voice mode button (the sound-wave icon next to the message box) and just talk. It answers out loud; talk again when it finishes. Handy under a truck or on the tractor.
  • Read an answer aloud: tap the speaker icon under any answer.
  • Change the voice: your picture → Settings → Audio. Try af_heart (default), am_michael, bm_george or bf_emma.

One-time setup on each phone or computer

Browsers only allow the microphone on secure (https://) pages, so each device has to trust the Ark's own certificate once. Speakers and typing work without this; only the microphone needs it. On the Ark itself, skip this and use http://localhost:3000.

  1. Download the certificate: ark-root-ca.crt (from this page, on the device you're setting up).
  2. Install and trust it:
    • iPhone / iPad: open this page in Safari and tap the link → Allow. Then Settings → Profile Downloaded → Install. Then Settings → General → About → Certificate Trust Settings and switch on Caddy Local Authority.
    • Android: Settings → Security (or "Security & privacy") → More security settings → Encryption & credentials → Install a certificate → CA certificate, then pick the downloaded file. (Menu names vary a little by phone.)
    • Windows: double-click the file → Install Certificate → Local Machine → Place all certificates in: Trusted Root Certification Authorities → Finish. Restart Chrome or Edge.
    • Mac: double-click the file to add it to Keychain Access, open Caddy Local Authority, expand Trust and set it to Always Trust.
    • Firefox (any computer): Settings → Privacy & Security → Certificates → View Certificates → Authorities → Import.
  3. Open Ask the Ark at https://ark.local:3443 (Android: https://<the Ark's IP address>:3443) and allow the microphone when the browser asks. Bookmark it or add it to your home screen.
Why trust it? The certificate was created by the Ark itself and only vouches for the Ark's own addresses. Its private key never leaves the Ark.

Data Toolkit

CyberChef · port 8084
Open ↗

A "Swiss Army knife" for data. Drag an operation from the left into the middle (the recipe), paste text into Input, and read the Output.

  • Convert units, dates and number formats; decode Base64, hex or Morse code.
  • Check whether a downloaded file is intact (SHA-256 checksum).
  • Encrypt or decrypt text with a shared password (AES) to pass private messages.

Regional Map

Offline map · port 8082
Open ↗

A detailed street map of the region chosen when the Ark was set up that works with no internet.

  • Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom. Zoom in far enough to see street names and buildings.
  • Use the style menu at the top to switch between dark, light and other map styles.
  • It shows the map but doesn't give turn-by-turn directions or your GPS position.

Mesh Radio

Meshtastic · port 8085  |  MeshCore · port 8086

Text messaging over small long-range LoRa radios, with no cell service or internet. The Ark supports both radio systems; they don't talk to each other, so use whichever one the family's radios run.

Meshtastic

  • Plug a Meshtastic radio into the Ark's USB port, then open http://localhost:8085 in Chromium on the Ark itself (Firefox and phones can't talk to USB radios).
  • Choose New connection → Serial and pick the radio.
  • Handheld radios can also be used straight from a phone with the Meshtastic app over Bluetooth.

MeshCore

  • Plug a MeshCore radio with Companion USB firmware into the Ark. It's picked up automatically.
  • Then open http://ark.local:8086 from any phone or laptop on the Wi-Fi to send and read messages.
Before a crisis: flash firmware, set the family channel and test the radios while the internet still works. Write the channel settings in Field Notes.

How-to guides

Add books to the Bookshelf

Kavita reads ePub, PDF, comic files (CBZ/CBR) and images. Ebooks with DRM (most Kindle and many store-bought ebooks) can't be opened; buy DRM-free copies when you can.

1. Put the file in a shelf folder

Each book gets its own folder inside a shelf, all under /srv/ark/books:

/srv/ark/books/
  Manuals/
    Where There Is No Doctor/
      Where There Is No Doctor.pdf
  • On the Ark itself (keyboard and screen, or Remote Desktop): open the Files app, go to /srv/ark/books, and drag the file into a shelf folder.
  • From another Linux PC: in its file manager, type sftp://data@ark.local/srv/ark/books in the address bar.
  • From Windows: use WinSCP (protocol SFTP, host ark.local, user data) and drag files into /srv/ark/books.

2. Tell Kavita about it

  • New book on an existing shelf: in Kavita go to Settings → Libraries and click the scan icon next to that library. It also rescans on its own every day.
  • New shelf: make the folder first. Then in Kavita go to Settings → Libraries → Add Library, name it, choose type Book (for PDFs and ePubs), add the folder /books/<Shelf name> and save.
Note: inside Kavita the folder is called /books/…; on the Ark's disk it's /srv/ark/books/…. They are the same place.

Add music

Navidrome plays MP3, Apple Lossless and AAC (.m4a), FLAC, OGG, Opus and WAV. Copy-protected files can't be played: old iTunes .m4p purchases and Apple Music subscription downloads.

  1. Copy the songs into /srv/ark/music, the same ways as books (the Ark's Files app, sftp://data@ark.local/srv/ark/music, or WinSCP). Any folder layout works; Artist/Album/ keeps things tidy.
  2. Wait a minute: Navidrome notices new files on its own. To check right away, sign in as the administrator and choose Scan library from the activity menu (top right).
Tip: Navidrome sorts by the tags inside each file (artist, album, track number, cover art), not by folder names. Files from iTunes and CD rips are normally tagged already.
Keep it private: our music is personal. It stays on the Ark and is never shared or published.

Add courses (Kolibri channels)

Needs the internet while importing. Afterwards the courses work offline.

  1. Sign in to Courses & Lessons as the administrator.
  2. Open the menu (☰) → Device → Channels → Import → Kolibri Studio.
  3. Pick a channel. Choose Import all, or open it and tick only the topics you want. The size is shown as you select.
  4. Click Import. Progress is shown under Device → Tasks; several imports queue up and run one after another.

Good picks: PhET Interactive Simulations (1 GB), CK-12 (7 GB), Open Stax college textbooks (2 GB), BCcampus trades textbooks (1 GB), Osmosis medical videos (5 GB), Sikana how-to videos (16 GB), Blockly Games (0.3 GB).

Teach the AI your documents

Give Ask the Ark a set of PDFs (manuals, family plans, medical guides) so it can quote them when it answers.

  1. In Ask the Ark, go to Workspace → Knowledge → + and create a collection, for example House Manuals.
  2. Drag PDFs or text files into it and wait for them to finish processing.
  3. In a chat, type # and pick the collection, or attach it to a model under Workspace → Models so it's always used.
Tip: ask it to "cite the document and page". If it can't point to a source, treat the answer as a guess.

Shut down or restart safely

  • Use the Mint menu → Shut Down or Restart, or press the power button once and choose Shut Down if asked.
  • Hold the power button for 5 seconds only if the Ark is frozen.
  • After it starts again, wait about 2 minutes. All sections come back on their own; nobody needs to log in.

FAQ

The dashboard won't load at all.
  • Make sure your device is on our Wi-Fi, not cellular data.
  • Type the address exactly: http://ark.local (http, not https).
  • Check the Ark is on (power light). After a power cut, give it about 2 minutes after it boots.
  • If it still won't load, the Ark may have a new network address. Someone with access can check it on the Ark with hostname -I.
One tile has a red or gray dot, or opens an error.

That section is stopped. Restarting the Ark fixes most problems. On the Ark, an adult can also open a terminal and run:

cd ~/ark && docker compose up -d

(Use the folder where the Ark project lives if it isn't ~/ark.)

Does the Ark need the internet?

No. Everything on it works fully offline. The internet is only needed to add things: downloading more courses, books or library updates, or new AI models.

What happens if the power goes out?
  • The Ark turns off with the power. Your content isn't harmed.
  • When power returns, it starts back up (if After Power Loss → Power On is set in its BIOS; otherwise press the power button).
  • A small battery backup (UPS) keeps it running through short outages.
Is it safe to trust the AI's answers?

Use it like a knowledgeable friend, not a doctor or engineer. It's great for explaining, brainstorming and summarizing, but it can be wrong. Check anything important in the Reference Library, especially medical and safety information.

Who can see what I type or read?
  • Nothing leaves the house: there's no cloud and no tracking.
  • Your AI chats and reading progress are tied to your own login.
  • Field Notes and these guide pages are open to anyone on our Wi-Fi. Keep private details (passwords, meeting places) off them.
I forgot my password.

Ask the family administrator (the parent who made the first account). In Ask the Ark it's under Admin Panel → Users; in the Bookshelf under Settings → Users; in Courses under Facility → Users.

How do I add more Reference Library books (Wikipedia with pictures, other sites)?

An adult, while the internet works: add a line to content/zims.list in the Ark project folder (the browse list is at download.kiwix.org/zim), then run scripts/download-zims.sh. It shows the sizes and asks first, resumes if interrupted, and refreshes the library when done.

How much room is left?

The dashboard's top bar shows free disk space next to the drive icon. The drive is 512 GB. The download scripts always keep at least 60 GB free so the Ark keeps running smoothly.

How do I keep it up to date?

Every few months, while the internet works, an adult runs these in the Ark project folder:

docker compose pull && docker compose up -d
scripts/download-zims.sh

The first updates the apps; the second fetches newer versions of the library books and removes the old ones.
